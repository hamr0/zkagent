## Findings

All observed on the Pixel 6a, stock Android 17, or on real public fixtures:

1. StrongBox available; keygen timings StrongBox 146/98 ms, TEE 142/36 ms;
   chain lengths StrongBox 4, TEE 5.
2. All four real chains verified with stdlib only; extension decoded fully
   (`attestationApplicationId` with package + signing digest = FR10
   identity; `verifiedBootState` Verified; `deviceLocked` true; patch levels
   present; challenge present). Risk #4 holds for parsing + signature
   verification.
3. Planted negatives fired: (i) flipped leaf signature →
   `signatureValid:false`; (ii) swapped root → `rootPinned:null`; (iii)
   chain evaluated with `--at` past the issuer's `notAfter` → expired.
4. Risk #8 CONFIRMED: diff of two fresh-key runs, same config: only
   `attestationChallenge` differs. Stable and device-correlated: all
   intermediate cert serials/validity, `verifiedBootKey`, `verifiedBootHash`,
   `osVersion`, os/vendor/boot patch levels. Cross-config (StrongBox vs TEE)
   `verifiedBootKey`/`Hash` and patch levels also identical. TEE cert 1 is an
   O=TEE cert with a 13-day validity window (2026-08-24→09-06) — RKP
   per-device provisioned; StrongBox certs 1–2 are 2020-09→2030-09 factory
   keybox certs. The raw attestation chain is a device identifier on both
   paths → tier A cannot carry it as-is.
5. Verifier gap found by reading dates by hand: the StrongBox chain roots
   in Google's 2016→2026-05-24 root — expired — and the verifier initially
   accepted it because `X509Certificate.verify()` checks signatures only.
   Fixed with `--at`/validity check. Today: both StrongBox chains FAIL
   (expired root), both TEE pass; at `--at 2026-05-01` the reverse (TEE cert
   1 "not yet valid"). No single date validates all four. Policy for
   pinned-but-expired roots is open (Q14 sub-item d); whether Google's own
   verifier ignores root expiry is UNVERIFIED.
6. Public `ec_strongbox` fixture rejected by the strict walk (intermediate
   re-issued under a new subject, same key) — structural, not substantive;
   not seen on the real chains.
7. Privacy defect in the M0 spike app: MRZ fields (document number, expiry,
   DOB) pre-filled on launch — upstream persists them in preferences,
   contradicting the M0 row's "typed by hand, never stored". Must not
   survive into M2.
8. Method: launcher activity is `.RegularActivity` (not `.MainActivity`);
   the first tap triggered an NFC read (document near phone) instead of the
   probe (TECH_DISCOVERED → ResultActivity). The phone was driven via the
   baremobile MCP (snapshot/tap) after that.
